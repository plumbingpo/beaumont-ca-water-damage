Wet Carpet Drying: What Beaumont Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For Beaumont homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

What It Looks Like

  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Stains From Furniture

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. Such spots are frequently there for good, while the legs soak up water and swell.

Damp Base of the Walls

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Water Held in the Pad

Pad can hold many times its weight in water. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

Water Quality Drops

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

Carpet Starts Failing

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

Dry It or Replace It?

We save what can be dried properly and remove only what cannot. Moisture readings make that call, not guesswork.
